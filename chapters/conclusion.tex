While this thesis only condenses the literature surrounding Comon's conjecture and attempts to generate counterexamples to it, the fact that a computed, real counterexample does exist provides some hope for a conclusive answer in the future. As mentioned in \cref{chapter:constructionsrequired}, since the real counterexample requires the discrepancy \cref{eqn:familyineq}, an equivalent condition in the complex case seems to be the pathway forward.

To be more precise, if a symmetric, complex-valued, order $d$ tensor $T$ exists, along with a family of symmetric, complex-valued, order $d-1$ tensors $\mathcal{M}$, such that the statement $$\min \rank T \mod \mathcal{M} < \min \srank T \mod \mathcal{M}$$ still holds, then a viable candidate counterexample can be constructed, paralelling the constructions in \cites{shitov_comons_2020, wang_lower_2023}, since the only part of these constructions dependent on the underlying field is the choice of candidate real $T_{\mathbb{R}}$ represented by the homogenous even order $d$ polynomial $x^d - ky^d$.
\section{Further Possible Work}\label{section:furtherwork}
The work in this thesis establishes a baseline for exploring constructions of candidate counterexamples to Comon's Conjecture. 
\begin{itemize}
    \item One way this work can be extended is to focus more on a geometric description of the constructions made in this paper, particularly for \cref{eqn:familyineq}. In doing so, the hope is that one would uncover geometric conditions that indicate a candidate counterexample to the conjecture, and these are hopefully independent of the base field chosen.
    \item A more explicit understanding of the choices of vectors made in \cref{subsection:emulatingfamily}. The choices made so far stem from \cite{shitov_comons_2020}, and while they have proved to be effective, have no indication as to why they are. Studying the construction of such a family more carefully might indicate algebraic or geometric conditions that are being met by the collection of slices, allowing for the existence of an emulating family of elementary tensors.
    \item Comon's conjecture as originally stated in \cite{oeding_report_2008} was only for $\reals$- or $\complex$-valued tensors. A conclusive understanding of whether Comon's conjecture can be extended to finite fields, or if such an extension is non-trivial can be one path forward. 
    \item For the real, even order case, the developement of explicit algorithms that compute the optimal rank decomposition for a given symmetric decomposition is another potential avenue of work. The guiding principle is of the Gram-Schmidt process that allows for any collection of $n$ vectors to be made into an orthonormal basis for an $n$ dimensional vector space.
    \item Adjusting the work in this paper to understand the smallest possible cloning operation required to satisfy the conditions of the emulating family could be another potential extension of this work. \cite{borovik_construction_2025} while not explicitly related to Comon's conjecture, does establish an upper bound for how large a cloning operation may be required.
    \item Since the relationship between symmetric tensors and homogenous polynomials is explicit, extending that mapping to show a general relationship between tensors having a certain order, and polynomials having the same degree. \cite{comon_symmetric_2008} indicates that such an extension may not be possible directly.
\end{itemize}
